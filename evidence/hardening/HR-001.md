# HR-001: reading level broke on almost every page (prompt p1 to p2)

Date: 10 Oct 2026. Promise: `age_reading_level` ("Sentences are short and the words are easy for this age"; deterministic check, limit 14 words per sentence at age 6).

## Trigger

The first full run and the first baseline sweep (`sweep-1-p1`) both showed `age_reading_level` failing on the first pass for nearly every page: the writer produced 15 to 22 word sentences against a 14 word limit. In sweep 1 it was the most common broken promise in both arms.

- Trace of one affected story (before): `264208295ada9b28a96d2e29b32fb41c` (bunny, sharing, B1, seed 1; 7 reading-level violations, 7 repairs, 358.6 s). Export: `evidence/traces/20261010-1508-IST-sweep1-p1-bunny-sharing-B1-7-reading-level-repairs.json`.

## Diagnosis

Hypothesis, written before the change: the writer is never told the number. The p1 prompts said "one or two short sentences using simple words" while the verifier enforced a hard per-age word limit, so the model had no way to know where the line was.

What the data show (pages, all runs at age 6, so 8 pages per story):

- B0 (one call, no gate) in sweep 1: 96 of 224 pages over the limit, and every one of them shipped.
- B1 (gated) in sweep 1: 181 of 200 pages over the limit on the first pass; the gate caught and repaired them (13 still shipped broken after the repair budget ran out), at the cost of 8.8 repairs per story.

The check is deterministic and the same in both arms, so the difference between the prompt and the check was the cause, not a verifier fault.

## Change

- Commit `04ffebb` (Sat 10 Oct 13:40 IST): state the per-age limit in `WRITE_SYSTEM` and `BASELINE_SYSTEM` (`{max_words}`, from the same table the verifier reads). Prompt version `p1` to `p2`. Nothing else in the writer changed for B0.
- Entire checkpoint: **none for this commit**. It was made in a session run from outside the repository, before Entire hooks were attached (see `HANDOFF.md`); we do not invent one. The regression test below was committed from a session inside the repository.

Reading the numbers honestly:

- B0 is the control: it has no gate and no pipelining, and only the prompt changed for it. Its drop from 42.9% to 1.8% is therefore attributable to the prompt.
- B1's drop from 90.5% to 9.8% is the same change, but B1 also changed in other ways between the sweeps (pipelined review, HR-002), so the B1 number alone does not prove the prompt did it.
- The gate still matters: after the fix B0 and B2 still ship 4 and 3 over-limit pages, B1 ships 0.
- B2 (the model reviewing its own story) did not catch the pages that B1's deterministic check caught: it shipped 3 broken reading-level pages and, over all promises, 27 of 28 stories with a broken promise, against 0 of 28 for B1.

## What is still wrong (not fixed here)

B1's page-by-page writer is over the limit on 9.8% of pages against 1.8% for the one-call baseline. In the after trace the written pages sit right at the limit ("longest 12 / 14 / 13 / 14 words"), and the failing page had a 15 word sentence, so the stated limit seems to become a target the writer overshoots by a word. This is a hypothesis from one trace; the repair fixed it in one attempt (8 words). Candidate next change, untested: give the writer a target a couple of words under the limit. If we try it, it becomes HR-003 with its own before/after.

All runs were age 6; other ages are covered only by the regression test, not by a run.
