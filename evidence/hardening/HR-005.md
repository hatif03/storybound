# HR-005: one Gemini call stalled for 87 s per trace (neatlogs triage card): tried a 40 s timeout, not kept

Date: 10 Oct 2026. Not a broken promise but latency: time lost to a model call that never answered.

## Trigger

neatlogs's automatic triage opened card `bdd1f6f1` ("Investigate Gemini 3.7 Flash 504 Timeout", severity high, confidence 0.65, "root cause not established"). Traces `050dc8b85289002f084c67e8cfc23a42` and `08177d773d0e88d8b26febc22898ba03`: exactly one `gemini-3.7-flash` call per trace ended in error after 87.6 s and 87.7 s, the retry succeeded, and the stall was about 40% of a 224-241 s run. Raw card: `evidence/neatlogs/20261010-1757-IST-triage-cards.json`; [TRIAGE-REVIEW.md](../neatlogs/TRIAGE-REVIEW.md).

## Diagnosis

The card's own first step was "check the configured timeout". Confirmed: every Gemini call used `request_timeout_s = 90`, while normal text and vision calls take 3-18 s. Hypothesis: a 40 s timeout for text and vision calls (images keep 90 s) would turn an 87 s stall into a 40 s one, because the retry already succeeds.

## Change and test

- Commit `7961904` (Entire checkpoint `01M4JW5VCR4C3HDMCZPFVGQ2K4`): `text_timeout_s = 40`, with an environment override `STORYBOUND_TEXT_TIMEOUT_S` so it could be tested.
- Test: the same 14 jobs (seed 1, B1, 7 drawings x 2 lessons) with 40 s (`sweep-5-hr45`) and straight afterwards with 90 s (`sweep-6-t90`). Calls that errored for 30 s or more were read from the neatlogs traces.

| | 40 s (sweep 5) | 90 s (sweep 6) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean / median / max seconds per story | 203.7 / 163.4 / 397.3 | 114.9 / 108.7 / 252.6 |
| Average successful Gemini call | 9.0 s | 4.2 s |
| Calls that errored for 30 s or more | 12 (all about 37 s, in 8 of 14 traces) | 0 |

## Result

**Not supported, so the default is back to 90 s** (this commit). With the shorter timeout 12 calls were killed at about 37 s and the stories were slower; with 90 s no call stalled at all. Two readings fit and we cannot separate them: the provider was slow during sweep 5 (the average successful call was twice as slow) and the 40 s limit killed calls that would have finished, or hangs only appear at some times. Sweep 4 (90 s, earlier) also had no stalls, so the 87 s hangs in the triage traces were not reproduced in any of the 42 jobs we ran afterwards. Without a reproduction there is no evidence the change helps, and some that it can hurt, so we do not keep it. If hangs return, the better fix is probably a hedged retry (start a second call after a short wait and take the first answer), which needs its own experiment.

What the card was right about: the stalls are real and the timeout was the place to look. What it did not show: that shortening the timeout is safe.

Sample sizes are 14 jobs per arm and outputs are sampled; the cost estimates and the provider's state at the time are not controlled.

## Regression fixture

None: nothing was kept. The override `STORYBOUND_TEXT_TIMEOUT_S` stays so the experiment can be repeated.
