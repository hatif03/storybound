# HR-008: we had never checked the judge for order and length bias, never frozen a past failure, and tuned and reported on the same drawings

Date: 11 Oct 2026. Found by reading two evaluation guides (SWE-bench, OpenAI's evaluation best practices; notes in [docs/research/evals-and-observability.md](../../docs/research/evals-and-observability.md)) against our own evidence, not by a failure.

## Trigger

Three gaps, each visible in our own files:
1. **Judge bias untested.** OpenAI's guide names position and verbosity bias as the two judge pitfalls. Our judge decides several promises in one call (`judge_batch`), so the order of the promises could matter, and a longer page could read as safer. Our 24/24 fault result (`scripts/eval_judge.py`) tested neither.
2. **Past failures were prose.** HR-001 to HR-007 each name a job to re-run, but nothing re-runs them all and reports a pass condition per case (SWE-bench's one-record-per-case idea).
3. **Tuning and reporting on the same data.** Prompts p1 to p5 were tuned on 7 drawings and every sweep reused them.

## Diagnosis

Hypotheses written before running: (1) some promise verdicts will flip with order or padding, most likely the borderline quality promises, not the safety ones; (2) a frozen set will show cases that still ship a break, because the earlier "0 of 28" was one sweep on one set of drawings; (3) holdout drawings will show a higher break rate than the tuning drawings.

## Change

- `scripts/judge_bias.py`: same page and promises in three orders, and with two harmless sentences added; clean pages and two injected faults (swapped answer, scary passage). Ledger `kind: judge-bias`.
- `scripts/regress.py` and `evidence/regression/cases.jsonl`: 16 cases frozen from the ledger (the first two failures of each promise family), each with the promise that broke (`must_flip`), the prompt version, the sweep and the trace. Outcomes kept apart: **resolved**, **unresolved** (shipped with a break), **error** (no result).
- `src/storybound/ledger.py`: drawings declared **tuning** (the 8 used in sweeps 1 to 7) or **holdout** (5 never used in a story run); every ledger row has `split`.
- Commit for `499 CANCELLED` (found by the regression run, below): `gemini._RETRYABLE` now includes 499; test `tests/test_basics.py::test_a_cancelled_vertex_call_is_retried`.

## Before and after

**Judge bias** (`evidence/runs/20261011-1050-IST-judge-bias-1.json`, source run `bunny-sharing-B1`, 8 pages x clean / swap / scary = 24 page runs, 72 promise verdicts, 0 judge errors):

| | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Clean pages, any order, any padding | **0 flips** |
| `story_safe_text` on the 8 scary pages | critical in every order and with padding |
| `correct_choice_teaches_lesson` on the swapped-answer pages | violation in every order and with padding |
| Verdicts that changed with the order | 6 of 72, all `lesson_concept_in_scene` on faulted pages |
| Verdicts that changed with two added sentences (14 words on average) | 3 of 72, all `lesson_concept_in_scene` on faulted pages |

The hypothesis held in shape: the borderline quality promise is the unstable one, the safety and fault-target promises did not move. Limits: one lesson, one source story, one judge, 14 words of padding (not a 2x length change), and the faulted pages are off-topic on purpose so "is the lesson in this scene" is a genuinely ambiguous question there.

**Frozen regression set** (`evidence/regression/20261011-1104-IST-regress-1.json`, current code, prompt p5, pictures off):

| Outcome | Cases |
| --- | --- |
| resolved (shipped, every promise kept) | 12 |
| unresolved (shipped with a break on the receipt) | **3**: rc-03 (honesty, bunny, 10 repairs; reading level three times and two lesson promises), rc-05 (honesty, copyrighted-character fixture, 14 repairs; reading level twice), rc-10 (honesty, same fixture; one lesson-specific visual-continuity promise) |
| error | 1: rc-08, Vertex `499 CANCELLED` under load |
| same promise broke again on the first pass | 2 of 16 |

Read this honestly. The 3 unresolved are the hardest cases, chosen because they failed once, so a higher break rate than the last full sweep (1 of 28 in `sweep-7-quiz`) is expected; it is still the real number to bring down, and the reading-level promise on honesty stories is the weak point that the earlier sweeps already named. The regression run was **confounded**: it ran at the same time as the judge-bias job (also on K2), a live browser story and three workers, and those jobs took 315 to 395 s instead of about 150 s, so more repairs or an exhausted repair budget may be load effects. Re-running rc-08 alone after the 499 change completed and shipped clean (`regress-1-rc08`); one run, no load, so it does not prove that the 499 retry was the cause. **The three unresolved cases were then re-run alone, one at a time (`regress-2-alone`): all three resolved** (0, 2 and 7 repairs; none broke the same promise on the first pass again except by repair). One run each, so this supports the load explanation but does not prove it; rc-10 still needed 7 repairs, which is the real cost of those cases. Counting the isolated re-runs, all 16 cases end resolved (12 in the busy run, 3 re-run alone, and rc-08 re-run after the 499 change).

**Holdout** (`holdout-1`, 5 never-tuned drawings x lessons sharing and honesty, gated arm, one story each, `split: holdout`):

| | Tuning (`sweep-7-quiz`, 28 stories) | Holdout (`holdout-1`, 10 stories) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stories with a break on the first pass | 20 of 28 | 9 of 10 |
| Repairs per story (mean) | 2.36 | 2.1 |
| Shipped with a broken promise | 1 of 28 | **0 of 10** |

The holdout drawings did **not** do worse: no sign that the prompts were overfitted to the tuning drawings. Ten stories is a small sample (one drawing, a pii-name fixture, needed 6 repairs), the holdout ran under load (mean 281 s per story against 154 s), and the lessons were the same two.
