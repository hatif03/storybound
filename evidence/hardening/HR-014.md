# HR-014: architecture and configuration experiments: which writer, judge and repair budget is best?

Date: 12 Oct 2026. Asked by the neatlogs team's note (try different configurations and architectures, and see whether ours is the best at what it does) and by our own dashboard, which showed few incidents.

## Trigger
Every earlier claim compared the agent with itself (gated against ungated). Nobody had asked whether a different writer, a second judge, no judge, or a different repair budget would do better on the same drawings.

## Diagnosis
Hypotheses written before the runs: (1) a stronger writer lowers repairs and cost per clean story is still higher than the cheap writer plus repair; (2) a second judge adds safety but not enough to pay for itself; (3) removing the judge saves time and lets breaks through unseen; (4) removing repairs ships the breaks the checks find; (5) a larger repair budget helps little.

## Change
No product code changed for the comparison itself. Tooling added: failure injection and configuration fields in the ledger, `scripts/run_experiments.py`, `scripts/compare_experiments.py`, `sweep.py --audit`, `STORYBOUND_MAX_REPAIRS`, `STORYBOUND_JUDGE_ENSEMBLE`, `STORYBOUND_ROUTES`. One fix came out of X2 (HR-015).

## Method
Ten held-out stories per configuration (five drawings the prompts were never tuned on, lessons sharing and honesty, gated arm, age 6), all run through `scripts/run_experiments.py`, each a tagged sweep (`experiment` in neatlogs, `experiment` and the full configuration in every `evidence/ledger.jsonl` row). The baseline is `holdout-routes-default` (HR-012). Results: [20261012-comparison.md](../experiments/20261012-comparison.md), made by `scripts/compare_experiments.py`. A second tool, `scripts/sweep.py --audit`, re-judges every page of every shipped story with the K2 judge and counts the breaks that remain (`kind: audit` rows).

## Before and after
The baseline is the "before"; each configuration is an "after".

| Configuration | Stories shipped | Shipped with a known break | Repairs a story | Seconds (mean) | Estimated $ a story |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline (Gemini Flash writer, K2 judge, 2 repairs) | 10 of 10 | 1 | 2.4 | 158 | 0.024 |
| X1 writer: Gemini 3.1 Pro | 10 of 10 | 0 | 1.3 | 213 | 0.113 |
| X2 writer: Claude Sonnet 4.6, first run | 7 of 10 (3 errors, see HR-015) | 1 | 2.7 | 138 | 0.091 |
| X2b writer: Claude Sonnet 4.6, after the parser fix | 10 of 10 | 0 | 5.1 | 218 | 0.122 |
| X3 judge ensemble (K2 and Sonnet, the stricter verdict wins) | 10 of 10 | 0 | 3.6 | 241 | 0.138 |
| X4 no judge (deterministic checks only) | 10 of 10 | 1 | 1.1 | 49 | 0.020 |
| X5 no repairs (the checks only observe what breaks) | 4 clean, 6 with notes | 6 | 0.4 | 154 | 0.018 |
| X6 four repairs a page instead of two | 10 of 10 | 0 | 2.5 | 177 | 0.022 |

The audit (the same ten drawings and lessons, 80 pages each, every page re-judged after shipping):

| Configuration | Pages with a break the shipped story still carries | Stories with at least one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A0 baseline | 16 of 80 | 6 of 10 |
| A5 no repairs | 37 of 80 | 7 of 10 |
| A4 no judge | 48 of 80 | 8 of 10 |

## What we conclude
- **The checks only keep promises if they can repair.** Observing without repairing shipped a known break in 6 of 10 stories (X5); the audit shows the same effect at page level. Detection is not the product, repair is.
- **The judge matters more than it looks.** Without it a story takes 49 s and ships; the audit says 48 of its 80 pages break a promise the run never saw. The "shipped a break" column of X4 (1) is the number the agent knows about, not the number that is true.
- **A better writer helps and costs a lot.** Gemini Pro needs about half the repairs (1.3 against 2.4) at 4.7 times the cost; Claude Sonnet needs more (5.1) at five times the cost, so on this task it is worse than Pro. The cheap writer plus repair reaches the same shipped result for a fifth of the price, so the default stays.
- **A second judge is safe and expensive:** no extra breaks shipped, 3.6 repairs a story, 5.7 times the cost. It is kept as an option (`STORYBOUND_JUDGE_ENSEMBLE`), not the default; it is also the failover for the K2 judge (HR-015).
- **Two repairs are close to enough, four are slightly better at no cost:** 10 of 10 clean at 2.5 repairs a story, against 9 of 10 at 2.4 in the baseline. The difference is one story, so this is a candidate for a default change, not a result; the default stays at two until it is repeated.

## Limits, said plainly
Ten stories per configuration, one run each, five drawings: a single story moves a percentage by ten points, and no confidence interval is claimed. The audit judge is the same K2 judge that gates, so the audit measures what the judge can see on a second look (and its own variation: 16 of 80 pages in the baseline were flagged on a re-judge after the gate passed them), not an independent human truth; the human check of the judge is the neatlogs evaluation in `evidence/neatlogs/review-3/`. Costs are estimates from measured tokens at list prices; the Sonnet and ensemble costs are priced at the dearest gateway rate.
